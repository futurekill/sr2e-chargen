# Rulings

Where the books are ambiguous, conflict, or leave a gap, the app follows the ruling here. Each one names the pages,
the reading chosen, and why. The user (the GM) can overturn any of them. **R1–R4 confirmed by the GM on 2026-11-18.**

| # | Topic | Pages | Ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Starting skill cap with a concentration | core p.45, p.70 | The cap of 6 applies to the **general** Skill Rating after p.70's reduction. A concentration or specialization may go above 6, so 7 points with a concentration (Firearms 6, Pistols 8) is legal; 7 points with no concentration (Firearms 7) is not. | p.45: "no starting character may have a Skill Rating of more than 6, unless Concentrations and Specializations are assigned". The general rating is the Skill Rating; the concentration and specialization ratings are the exception the sentence allows. Decided 2026-11-18 (M2b code review); confirmed by the GM 2026-11-18. |
| R2 | Which language is the free local dialect | core p.46 | Any language the player names may be marked as the free Street dialect; the app does not keep a list of dialects. | The book gives examples ("City Speak or Tunnel Talk"), not a list; which dialect is local is the GM's call. |
| R3 | Magic priority buys exactly one type | core p.45–46 | The Magic priority buys the table's type exactly: a human magician A, a human adept B, a metahuman magician B, a metahuman adept C (More Metahumans: A magician, B adept). A higher letter does not buy the lower type. | p.45: "Human adepts must assign Priority B to Magic, and metahuman adepts must assign Priority C". |
| R4 | Improved Physical Attributes tier | core p.125 | Each +1 is priced by the attribute's NATURAL rating (bought + racial modifier) plus the improvement so far, against the racial maximum. Cyberware, bioware and spells do not move the tier. One power per attribute. | p.125 prices by "how much higher (or lower) than the character's Racial Maximum is the final attribute rating"; the racial maximum is a natural-rating limit, so the natural rating is the one compared. Decided 2026-11-18 (M2c-2 plan review); confirmed by the GM 2026-11-18. |
| R5 | Weapon focus: size and the weapon | core p.137, p.147, p.263 | The bond costs 4 × Force for a small weapon focus and 5 × Force for a large one. A weapon is **small when its Concealability is above 5**; Concealability 5 or less, or a weapon that cannot be concealed (99 in the data), is large. The focus bonds to one melee weapon the character buys (one focus per weapon, quantity 1), and its price is p.263's [(Reach + 1) × 100,000¥] + Force × 90,000¥ from that weapon's Reach. The weapon's own list price is paid too, as Foundry does. A physical adept is a magician (p.124), so it gets Force Points (p.46) and can bond a weapon focus at creation, its only useful focus. | p.137's Focus Bonding Table lists "Weapon Focus, Small" and "Weapon Focus, Large" with no definition; how easy the weapon is to conceal is the GM's measure of its size. p.147 limits weapon foci to melee weapons. Size rule set by the GM 2026-11-18. |
| R6 | Selling gear after creation | core p.188 | **A GM setting** (decided by the GM 2026-11-19): **fence** — the book's rule: 30% of the item's list value, moved 5% per extra Negotiation success, never below 10% or above 50% (the player enters the agreed percentage) — or **refund** — what was paid comes back (Foundry's long-standing house simplification). The app and Foundry share the setting's meaning and the price function. | p.188 prices loot through a fence; Foundry's tables had been playing with a full refund. The GM chooses per campaign. |
| R7 | Buying gear after creation | core p.184 | The price shown is the street price (list × Street Index, as Foundry charges in play); the player may enter the negotiated price (the book's ±5% per extra success) before buying, and the GM checks it. Availability tests stay at the table. | p.184: legal, available gear sells at list; the shadow and gray markets at list × Street Index, then negotiated. The app can't know whether a purchase was legal or how the dice fell, so the player enters the result. Decided by the GM 2026-11-19. |
| R8 | Damage, healing and surgery after creation | core p.111, p.114; Cybertechnology p.53, p.57 | The app records wounds and rounds, not dice: damage and healing are box counts. **Healing removes the Physical overflow first**, then the Physical column. Damage and ammunition use are not history entries (no Undo: heal or buy instead); the document's revisions keep them. Surgery uses Foundry's rules: Cybertechnology p.53's Essence change from the surgeon's successes (2 successes = no change, the core book's result) and p.114's categories for the wound and fee (the fee and the wound can each be left off). Muscle Replacement beside full cyberlimbs costs 20% less Essence per limb (p.57), as Foundry already counts it. | p.111 says overflow is the worst damage (a character dies past it), so it heals first. Wounds change every session; a history entry per box would bury the money and Karma entries. Decided 2026-11-19 (M4c plan). |
| R9 | Metavariants | Companion p.39–44 | A variant is its base metatype with the listed exceptions: the **same Race priority**, the **base metatype's racial maximums** (the only maximum the book names, the Cyclops' Strength 10, is the troll's), its own modifiers and abilities. Every variant's Hostile reaction, the Dryad's and Night One's Mild allergies and the Dryad's limited Animal Empathy are **notes** for the GM, not automated; the Cyclops' +2 TN on ranged attacks is automated in Foundry (thrown weapons included). The magical oni's Race B (Resources A, Magic C) is a **GM option**, off by default, and requires a physical adept (Magic C buys a metahuman adept, core p.45). | p.39 gives exceptions to "the standard … racial modifications" and no priority change; p.42 puts the oni option "at the gamemaster's discretion". Decided 2026-11-19 (M5b plan). |
| R10 | A shapeshifter's starting Karma Pool | core p.47; Companion p.36–38 | **1 point** (the standard), with or without More Metahumans. | p.47 gives the second point to *metahumans* ("unless the More Metahumans optional rule … in which case they only get the standard 1 point"); a shapeshifter is not a metahuman (governments deny them metahuman status, Companion p.38) and the Companion grants nothing. Foundry gave 2 before (it counted every non-human as a metahuman); changed with this ruling. Decided 2026-11-19 (M5c plan); the GM may overturn. |
| R11 | Edges and Flaws at creation | Companion p.22–35 | Offered when the Companion is on (the book's optional rule; the GM decides by turning the book on). The values are the p.35 table's; **Addiction** takes −1 to −4, the table's components summed (Strongly Addictive −1, Debilitating −1, Incapacitating −2), though its text says "−1 or −2"; "Variable" values are any whole value, Edges 1–6, Flaws −1 to −6 (p.23's limits). Each quality is taken once — per attribute for the attribute Edges, per subject for Aptitude, Incompetence, Allergy, Phobia, Compulsive, Addiction and Dependent ("unless otherwise noted, Edges and Flaws cannot be combined", p.23). Physical adepts count as magically active. | The table and the text disagree on Addiction; the table lists the components the text describes. Decided 2026-11-19 (M5d plan); the GM may overturn. |
| R12 | Point-based design | Companion p.20–23; core p.46–47 | The book's costs; a physical adept gets no Force Points under points (p.21, unlike R5's priority reading); a magician sells at most his 30 Force Points (−15 BP); an allergy's and Bonus Skill/Force Point's free points reduce the Building Points owed (they grant "extra" points in their category); the starting Karma Pool follows p.47 as written whatever the method; a shapeshifter and magic types the table doesn't price (Grimoire's adepts) need the priority method. | The book gives the table and the 30 Force Points but not every interaction. Decided 2026-11-19 (M5h plan). |
| R13 | The synaptic accelerator beside other reflex ware | Shadowtech p.28; Cybertechnology p.57 | At creation the app follows the books where Foundry only warns: a synaptic accelerator with **wired or boosted reflexes** is an error (Shadowtech: "incompatible … cannot be combined"); with **move-by-wire** only Level 1, and the accelerator costs **triple** (both are implanted together at creation, as p.57 requires). After creation the same are warnings. Foundry counts every Initiative die and prices the accelerator normally, so a character made elsewhere may hold such a combination; the GM decides. | The books are explicit; the system's rules don't encode them yet (a system change is proposed separately). Decided 2026-11-19 (M5k plan). |
| R14 | The Toxin Exhaler's toxin | Shadowtech p.32, p.96–100; Corporate Security Handbook p.81 | The toxin is chosen at creation from the catalog's compounds that **deal damage and are priced per dose**: Cyanide, Hyper, MAO, Atropine (Shadowtech) and Gamma-Scopolamine (CSH, with that book on). Not Doom or Gamma-Anthrax (a virus and a bacterium, not a toxin the sac's cells can make), not Kamikaze (no damage), not the MAO Injector (priced per six doses). The price is 30,000¥ + 100 × the dose price, and the cultured grade's ×4 applies to that whole price (it is the implant's price). The immunity and the attack are the GM's in play (Foundry keeps them in the notes). | p.32 says "a single specific toxin of the recipient's choice" with no list; a fixed list keeps the price checkable. Decided 2026-11-20 (M5m). |
| R15 | The built-in device | core p.261; Cybertechnology p.98 | The device is one of the character's own gear items, **one** of it, with a flat price (not a chip, drug, deck, service, papers, explosive or vehicle, and not gear priced per rating: build those in in Foundry). It goes **inside** the limb option, as an eye accessory goes inside a cybereye: the export has no separate gear item, and the option carries the device's normal price, which Foundry multiplies by 4 ("4 × Normal Cost"). The option's notes name the device. | The book gives only the price formula; holding the device inside the option keeps one price, paid once, and Foundry's own pricing agrees. Decided 2026-11-25 (M5m). |
| R16 | The simple replacement limb | core p.249; Cybertechnology p.49, p.98 | Plain cyberware: 1 Essence, 50,000¥, and **no** cyberlimb rules — no limb type or side, no attribute averages, no pair Body, no options. Cybertechnology p.49: "simple replacement limbs with no special capabilities do exist … The following rules apply to the latter type" (the obviously cybernetic kind). Foundry already gives an untyped limb no limb rule, so the two agree. Core p.249's "each pair of limbs replaced counts as one level of dermal armor" is replaced by Cybertechnology's rule, which counts only cyberlimbs (its newer material takes precedence, p.42). | p.49 is explicit; read 2026-11-25 (M5m). |
| R17 | A garage vehicle's price | core p.251, p.264 | The **whole** vehicle is the price a character pays when it buys a garage vehicle at creation: its list price, the Harley Scorpion's 1,000¥ per firmpoint fitted, each mounted weapon at **twice** its list price (a core Vehicle Weapon — Autocannon, Vehicle Launcher, Water Cannon, p.253 — at its list price: it is built for a mount, so nothing is adapted; sr2e wiki Rulings, 0.167.0), and its cargo at list price × quantity. A weapon or item on the vehicle belongs to the vehicle only, so it is paid once. A catalog vehicle bought straight into the character (S9) stays at its list price. A vehicle with no list price ("Restricted") can't be bought at creation, whatever is mounted on it. | p.251: "Weapons must be purchased separately. Adaptation of a weapon for a vehicle mount doubles its cost"; one owner per item avoids paying twice. |
| R18 | Which weapons go on which mount, and how many mounts | core p.251–253 | As the system rules them (sr2e 0.166.0, the wiki's Rulings page): "can mount" / "up to" / "one or two" = up to that many; the Stallion's armed variant is another vehicle; hardpoints take the MMG, HMG, Panther Assault Cannon and the Missile/Rocket Launcher (missiles and rockets are cargo); firmpoints take any firearm, the Ingram Valiant LMG included. | One ruling, shared with Foundry. |
